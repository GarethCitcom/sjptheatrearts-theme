Joining is three steps

Tell us about your child, we suggest a class, they come and try it. Nothing is paid and nothing is signed until you are sure.

Start my enquiry Read the FAQs
1

Tell us about your child

Their age, what they are drawn to and whether they have danced before. If you have no idea which class to pick, say so; that is the most common answer we get.

Takes about two minutes.

2

We reply with options

Lottie reads every enquiry and comes back with two or three classes that would suit, the times they run, the fee and the next available start date.

A real person, not an automated reply.

3

Come and try it

A free trial class in normal clothes. If it is right, you register through the member portal. If it is not, that is genuinely fine and we will suggest something else.

No pressure at the end of it.

The first class

What actually happens on the day

Arrive five minutes early. A teacher will meet you at the door, take your child in and introduce them to two or three others. Parents can stay and watch or come back at the end; both are normal.

What to wear

Anything they can move in, hair tied back, bare feet or trainers. No uniform for a trial.

What to bring

A named water bottle. That is the whole list.

If they are nervous

Tell us beforehand. We will pair them with a student who has been there a while.

Afterwards

The teacher will find you for two minutes and tell you honestly how it went.

New families

Questions we get asked most

My child has never danced. Will they be behind?

No. Classes are grouped by age and stage, and beginners join a class where other people are also learning it for the first time. If a class has moved on too far, we will put your child in a different one rather than let them struggle.

Is the atmosphere competitive?

Only if a student chooses it. The troupes compete and take it seriously. The rest of the school is recreational, and the two sit side by side without anyone feeling second-best.

Can I stay and watch?

During a trial, yes. After that we usually ask parents to wait outside the studio, because most children concentrate better without an audience. We run watching weeks so you can see progress.

My child has additional needs. What are the options?

Group classes, one-to-one lessons, or a mix of both. Get in touch and tell us as much or as little as you want to; we will talk through what has worked for other families before you commit to anything.

What happens after I send the form?

Lottie reads it and replies with class options, times, the fee and a trial date. Once you have had the trial and want to go ahead, you register through the member portal, which is where medical details, consents and payment are handled securely.

Do you take teenagers who are starting late?

Yes, and more often than you would think. Commercial and musical theatre are the usual starting points for a 13 or 14-year-old who has never had a lesson.

Still deciding?

Ring the question past us before you fill anything in. We would rather answer it than have you guess.
