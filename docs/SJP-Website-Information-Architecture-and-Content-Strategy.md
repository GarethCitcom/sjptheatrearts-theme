# SJP Theatre Arts
## Website information architecture and content strategy

Prepared for the redesign of **sjptheatrearts.co.uk**

---

## 1. Strategic recommendation

The new website should help a parent answer five questions, in this order:

1. Is SJP right for my child?
2. Which classes suit their age, interests and experience?
3. When are the classes and what do they cost?
4. Is the school safe, welcoming and credible?
5. What exactly do I do next?

The current site contains most of the right proof—professional teaching experience, an inclusive ethos, examinations, performances, testimonials and a wide class range—but the information is spread across “About”, “Members”, “Classes”, “Timetable”, “News” and the external booking journey. New families should not have to understand the school’s internal structure before they can find a suitable class.

The redesign should therefore be organised around **finding a class and joining SJP**, with the booking portal treated as the final step rather than the starting point.

### Recommended positioning

> **A welcoming place to find your confidence, develop your talent and love performing.**

SJP should feel:

- Ambitious, without being intimidating
- Professional, without feeling corporate
- Inclusive, without sounding generic
- Family-friendly, while giving teenagers their own sense of independence
- Rooted in Bromsgrove, with the energy of a live theatre school

### Primary conversion

**Find a class → book a trial or enquire → register through the booking portal**

If SJP does not offer trials, replace “Book a trial” with **“Join a class”** throughout. Avoid “Become a member” unless membership is a defined product with clear benefits, fees and terms.

## 2. Audience and content priorities

### Primary audience: parents and carers

Typical needs:

- A suitable class for their child’s age
- Confidence that beginners will be welcomed
- A safe, supportive environment
- Clear times, location, prices and clothing requirements
- Proof that teachers are qualified and DBS checked
- An easy, low-risk first step

Likely concerns:

- “Will my child fit in?”
- “Are they too young, too old or too inexperienced?”
- “Is the atmosphere competitive?”
- “What will this cost beyond the class fee?”
- “Do we have to commit before trying it?”
- “What happens after I submit the form?”

### Secondary audience: teenagers

Teenagers may arrive through social media, search independently and influence the final choice. Their route should speak directly to them rather than only to their parents.

Typical needs:

- Classes that feel age-appropriate
- Visible progression and performance opportunities
- Credible, industry-experienced teachers
- A sense of the people and atmosphere
- Options for both recreational and more serious training
- Quick access to timetable and social media

Recommended message:

> **Train your way.** Build confidence, sharpen your technique and perform with people who love it as much as you do.

### Tertiary audiences

- Existing families looking for the portal, timetable or notices
- Adults looking for ballet, ballroom or other available classes
- Prospective students seeking vocational preparation
- Schools, colleges and event partners
- People enquiring about private lessons, parties or wedding dance lessons

These audiences should be supported without dominating the first-time family journey.

### Navigation decisions

- Replace the ambiguous **“Members”** label. It makes useful information appear restricted to existing families.
- Keep the booking portal as **“Member Login”** in the utility area. For new families, link to it only after class selection or an enquiry.
- Combine timetable and fees. Families judge schedule and affordability together.
- Rename “Gallery” as part of **“Performances”** so images prove the student experience instead of acting as an isolated photo archive.
- Keep “News” only if SJP can publish at least once per term. Otherwise use a small “Latest from SJP” social section and current event cards.
- Remove “Shop” from the primary navigation unless it is commercially active and regularly maintained.
- Parties and wedding lessons can live under a small “More from SJP” footer group or have direct campaign pages; they should not complicate the core children-and-teen journey.

## 4. Core user journeys

### Journey A: parent who does not know which class to choose

**Home → Find a Class → choose age → choose interest → compare suitable classes → book a trial/enquire → confirmation**

The class finder should ask no more than:

- Student age
- Interests: dance, singing, acting, acro or “not sure”
- Experience: first class, some experience or advanced
- Preferred day, if useful

Results should show:

- Class name and plain-English description
- Age or stage
- Day, time and duration
- Teacher
- Price
- Availability
- “Book a trial” or “Ask us about this class”

### Journey B: parent who already knows the discipline

**Search or Classes → class detail → timetable & fee → book a trial/enquire**

Each class page must answer:

- Who is it for?
- What happens in the class?
- What will the student learn?
- Do they need experience?
- What should they wear or bring?
- When is it?
- What does it cost?
- Who teaches it?
- What can it lead to?

### Journey C: teenager arriving from social media

**Teen landing section → watch/see real students → browse teen classes → progression and performance opportunities → timetable → book/enquire**

Use direct language and visually distinguish teen content from early-years content. Avoid making all teen imagery feel like a children’s school.

### Journey D: existing family

**Header utility link → Member Login**

Existing-family tasks should never compete with the new-family conversion path.

## 5. Page-by-page content blueprint

## Home

### Purpose

Establish fit, trust and momentum within one scroll, then guide visitors into the class finder.

### Recommended section order

1. **Hero**
   - Headline: **Find their place to shine.**
   - Supporting copy: “Dance, sing and perform in Bromsgrove with welcoming, industry-trained teachers. Classes for children and teens of every ability.”
   - Primary CTA: **Find the right class**
   - Secondary CTA: **View timetable & fees**
   - Compact proof: “All abilities welcome · DBS-checked teachers · Shows and examinations”

2. **Choose by age**
   - Early Years
   - Children
   - Teens
   - Adults, only if strategically important

3. **Why families choose SJP**
   - Confidence and belonging
   - Professional tuition
   - Inclusive teaching
   - Real performance opportunities

4. **Popular classes**
   - Six cards maximum
   - Include age, next session and a clear detail link

5. **The SJP experience**
   - Short, energetic video or photo sequence
   - Message: classes, friendships, examinations and live performances

6. **A direct welcome from SJ**
   - 80–120 words, first person
   - Link to story and teaching team

7. **Parent and student testimonials**
   - Pair a parent quote with a teen/student quote
   - Use names only with permission

8. **Trust and accreditation**
   - Awarding bodies, safeguarding, qualified teachers
   - Explain what the marks mean; do not rely on logos alone

9. **Simple joining steps**
   - Find a class
   - Book a trial or ask a question
   - Register and get ready

10. **Visit SJP**
    - Address, map, parking guidance and studio photo

11. **Final CTA**
    - “Not sure where to begin? Tell us a little about your child and we’ll recommend a class.”

## Classes hub

Start with age routes, then allow filtering by discipline. Each card should use one consistent template.

Recommended filters:

- Age
- Discipline
- Experience
- Day

Do not ask visitors to open thirteen separate pages before they know whether a class is suitable.

## Class detail template

1. Class name and age range
2. One-sentence outcome
3. Authentic class photo or short clip
4. Who it is for
5. What students do and learn
6. Timetable, duration and fee
7. Teacher card
8. What to wear and bring
9. Examinations, performances or progression
10. Related classes
11. Persistent CTA

Sample copy:

> **Commercial Dance for ages 11–16**  
> High-energy choreography, musicality and performance skills in a friendly class where individuality is encouraged.

## Timetable & Fees

The timetable must be readable on a phone and available as structured webpage content—not only an image or PDF.

Include:

- Filters by age, discipline and day
- Studio name
- Start and finish time
- Teacher
- Term dates
- Price per class or term
- Trial policy
- Sibling, multi-class or payment-plan information
- Link from every row to the relevant class

Place full terms and conditions behind a clear link. Show the few rules that affect initial decisions in plain language.

## Join SJP

This is the reassurance and conversion page.

Recommended copy structure:

1. “Joining is easy”
2. Three-step process
3. Trial or enquiry policy
4. What happens after the form is sent
5. What the first class feels like
6. Clothing and equipment
7. Fees and commitment
8. Parent FAQs
9. Enquiry/booking form

Recommended form fields:

- Parent/carer name
- Student name
- Student age or date of birth
- Email
- Phone
- Class of interest or “please recommend”
- Experience and support needs, optional
- Preferred day, optional
- Consent checkbox and privacy link

Never ask for safeguarding or medical detail in an open marketing form. Collect sensitive information through the appropriate secure registration process.

Confirmation message:

> Thanks—your enquiry is with the SJP team. We’ll reply within **[confirmed response time]** with the best class options and the next available start date.

## About SJP

Lead with the school’s promise and values, then tell the founder story. The current biography is credible, but it should support the family decision rather than dominate the homepage.

Suggested sections:

- What SJP believes
- A welcome from SJ
- School story
- Meet the teachers
- Qualifications and professional experience
- Inclusion and additional-needs support
- Safeguarding
- Examinations, progression and alumni outcomes

## Performances

Use performance content as evidence of opportunity and belonging.

Include:

- Annual shows
- Workshops and guest choreographers
- Community and external performances
- Examination/medal moments
- Curated gallery
- Upcoming event only when date, location and booking status are current

Avoid a large unlabelled image grid. Use short captions that explain what the visitor is seeing.

## Contact & Visit

Include:

- Full address
- Embedded map
- Parking and arrival instructions
- Accessible entrance/facilities information
- Phone, email and monitored social links
- Studio opening/contact hours
- Expected response time
- General enquiry form

## 6. Content messaging system

### Message hierarchy

1. **Belonging:** Every student should feel welcomed and seen.
2. **Growth:** SJP builds confidence, technique and performance skills.
3. **Credibility:** Teaching is led by qualified, industry-trained professionals.
4. **Opportunity:** Students can enjoy classes, examinations, workshops and shows.
5. **Choice:** There is a route for beginners, recreational students and ambitious performers.
6. **Ease:** Finding and joining a class is straightforward.

### Tone of voice

Use:

- Warm, encouraging and specific
- Short sentences and active verbs
- “Your child” on parent-led pages
- “You” on teen-led sections
- Plain-English class descriptions
- Confident claims supported by proof

Avoid:

- Generic claims such as “the best tuition” without evidence
- Too many exclamation marks
- Industry terminology before it is explained
- Language that implies students must be naturally talented
- Copy that addresses only girls
- Calling every visitor a “member” before they have joined

### Reusable message lines

- **Find their place to shine.**
- **All the ambition. None of the pressure to fit a mould.**
- **First steps, big stages and everything in between.**
- **Professional training. Family-school warmth.**
- **Not sure which class fits? We’ll help you choose.**

## 7. Visual and brand direction

### Creative direction: “Backstage warmth, centre-stage energy”

Retain the recognisable purple and orange but use them with more restraint. The website should feel bright and lively without giving every class a different competing colour.

Recommended palette:

| Role | Colour | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Deep plum | `#381064` | Navigation, headings, primary buttons |
| Performance orange | `#FE7300` | Highlights, active states, graphic accents |
| Soft peach | `#DDB18F` | Warm backgrounds and supporting detail |
| Warm cream | `#FFF9F4` | Main light background |
| Pale lilac | `#F4EEF8` | Section separation |
| Ink | `#211B27` | Body copy |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards and reverse content |

Accessibility note: orange should not be used for small text on white. Use deep plum for readable text and buttons; orange works best as an accent or on large, high-contrast elements.

### Typography

- Display/headings: **Poppins**
- Body/UI: **DM Sans**
- Avoid script fonts in page headings; the logo already carries a decorative personality.

### Graphic language

- Large editorial crops of real students and teachers
- Rounded but not overly “childlike” cards
- Cropped arcs or stage-light shapes derived from the SJP monogram
- Subtle motion inspired by rehearsal marks, curtains and spotlights
- Clean iconography for age, time, level and location
- A consistent orange accent rather than rainbow colour-coding every discipline

### Photography principles

- Lead with authentic SJP photography
- Balance in-class reassurance with on-stage aspiration
- Show a range of ages, abilities and disciplines
- Include teachers interacting with students
- Use captions and meaningful alt text
- Confirm current student/parent media consent before launch
- Avoid stock photography where an authentic equivalent exists

## 9. Search and discoverability strategy

### Core search themes

- Dance school Bromsgrove
- Children’s dance classes Bromsgrove
- Teen dance classes Bromsgrove
- Theatre school Bromsgrove
- Ballet classes Bromsgrove
- Commercial dance Bromsgrove
- Tap classes Bromsgrove
- Acro classes Bromsgrove
- Singing and acting classes Bromsgrove
- Performing arts classes for children and teens

### Page-level SEO approach

- Give every discipline a useful, unique class page.
- Include age range, location, teacher and outcome in natural language.
- Use descriptive page titles, such as:
  - “Children’s Dance Classes in Bromsgrove | SJP Theatre Arts”
  - “Teen Commercial Dance Classes in Bromsgrove | SJP”
- Keep name, address and phone details consistent across the site and local listings.
- Add structured data for LocalBusiness/PerformingArtsTheater where appropriate, plus Event data only for current public events.
- Use descriptive image names and alt text based on the activity, not “gallery-pic-4”.
- Redirect retired URLs to the closest new equivalent.

### Sustainable content

Prioritise evergreen answers over a high-maintenance blog:

- How to choose a dance class for your child
- What to expect at a first dance class
- Dance classes for teenagers: beginner to advanced
- What to wear to ballet, tap, commercial and acro
- Recreational versus examination dance
- How performance builds confidence

Publish current events only when someone owns the update process.

## 10. Content audit: retain, rewrite, retire

### Retain and strengthen

- Inclusive, family-friendly positioning
- Founder’s professional background
- Qualified and DBS-checked teaching team
- Examinations and recognised bodies
- Annual shows and workshop opportunities
- Additional-needs tuition and participation
- Parent and student testimonials
- Bromsgrove location and parking guidance

### Rewrite or restructure

- Homepage founder biography
- Class descriptions and age eligibility
- Teacher biographies
- FAQs
- Timetable and booking instructions
- Testimonials, shortened around a clear outcome
- Qualification and safeguarding explanations

### Retire or urgently review

- COVID-specific FAQ unless there is a current policy
- 2022/2023 event notices and ticket pop-ups
- Placeholder “Lorem Ipsum” event content
- Duplicate teacher names and repeated content
- Old teacher profiles
- “Subscribe for latest update” if newsletters are not sent
- News content that visually presents the school as inactive
- Broken or unclear booking links
- Copyright year that appears abandoned

## 13. Measures of success

Track:

- “Find a Class” clicks
- Class-finder completion
- Timetable interactions
- Trial/enquiry form starts and completions
- Click-through to booking portal
- Confirmed registrations
- Phone, email and map actions
- Conversion by age group and class
- Mobile versus desktop completion
- Most common “not sure” enquiry themes

The most important measure is not general traffic. It is the percentage of suitable visitors who move from **interest → matched class → completed enquiry or registration**.

---

## Final design principle

The site should never make a family hunt for the practical details while trying to decide whether they belong.

Every major page should end by answering:

> **Is this right for us—and what do we do next?**
